RAG Document Chunking and Context Optimization Guide

Section 1: Chunking Granularity and Window Strategies

Document chunking is the process of breaking large text corpora into discrete, semantically coherent segments suitable for vector embedding and LLM context injection. The choice of chunk size directly impacts both retrieval precision and generation groundedness. Small chunks (100 to 300 characters) offer high semantic precision: the embedding represents a single concept with minimal noise, making it easy to achieve high cosine similarity. However, small chunks often lose surrounding context, leading to incomplete answers during generation. Large chunks (800 to 2000 characters) preserve rich surrounding context and multi-sentence arguments, but dilute the vector representation because the embedding model must compress multiple disparate ideas into a single fixed-dimensional vector. For technical and engineering documents, a chunk size of 400 to 600 characters strikes the empirical sweet spot between semantic focus and contextual completeness.

Section 2: Chunk Overlap Mechanics and Boundary Preservation

Chunk overlap (typically 10% to 20% of the chunk size) prevents critical information from being split across chunk boundaries. When a factual statement spans two paragraphs, splitting strictly at fixed character counts can cut a sentence in half, causing both chunks to fail vector matching. A default overlap of 50 characters ensures that boundary tokens are duplicated in adjacent chunks, preserving semantic continuity without causing excessive vector index bloat. Idempotency is preserved by calculating SHA-256 hashes on the composite tuple of (doc_id, chunk_index, chunk_text), ensuring that repeated re-ingestion does not pollute the vector store with duplicate entries.

Section 3: Context Window Sizing and Anti-Hallucination Guardrails

Injecting irrelevant chunks into the LLM context prompt significantly increases the risk of hallucination and 'lost-in-the-middle' attention degradation. Top-k retrieval should be constrained (typically k=3 to 5) to only the most relevant passages. Furthermore, production RAG services must enforce a strict similarity threshold (e.g. cosine similarity >= 0.25). When the top retrieved chunk fails to meet this threshold, the system must trigger an explicit fallback response: 'I do not have sufficient information in the provided context to answer this question.' This deterministic fallback stops the generator from fabricating plausible-sounding hallucinations when user queries are out-of-domain.
